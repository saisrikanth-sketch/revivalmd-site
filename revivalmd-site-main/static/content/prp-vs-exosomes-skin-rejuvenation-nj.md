## What Is PRP Therapy and How Does It Work?

PRP therapy uses platelet rich plasma taken from your own blood. The blood is spun to concentrate platelets, then used as a prp treatment on the skin or scalp.

Platelets release growth factors that signal tissue regeneration, collagen production, and cellular regeneration. According to the American Academy of Dermatology, growth factors play a role in skin repair and inflammation control (AAD, 2023).

PRP treatment is often paired with [microneedling](/how-maintain-healthy-facial-skin/). Tiny channels allow growth factors to reach skin cells more directly. A prp injection may also be used for hair loss to stimulate the hair follicle and support hair regrowth.

PRP treatment is part of regenerative medicine because it uses the body’s own healing systems. Many patients choose it for [acne scars](/rf-microneedling-rahway-nj-skin-tightening-guide/), texture, and hair growth support.

## What Is Exosome Therapy?

Exosome therapy uses microscopic messengers called extracellular vesicles. These vesicles come from stem cell sources, often mesenchymal stem cell lines, and carry bioactive molecules.

Exosomes help with cellular communication. They deliver growth signals, proteins, and genetic material that guide repair. Think of them as a regenerative signal that tells cells what to do next.

Exosome treatment does not use your blood. That’s appealing for some patients. Exosomes therapy is often chosen for calming inflammation, supporting rejuvenation, and improving recovery after procedures.

Research suggests exosomes support tissue regeneration and skin barrier repair (Journal of Cosmetic Dermatology, 2024). This area of regenerative medicine is still growing, but results look promising.
