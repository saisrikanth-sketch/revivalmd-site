## What Is TMJ Disorder and Why It Causes Pain

TMJ disorder affects the temporomandibular joint, the hinge that connects your jaw to your skull. This joint works every time you chew, talk, or yawn. When the muscles around it overwork, pain tends to follow.

Clenching and grinding, also called bruxism, are common triggers. Stress plays a role too. Over time, these habits strain the jaw muscle and nearby facial muscles. TMJ symptoms can include jaw pain, clicking, headaches, ear pressure, and neck tension. Some patients even notice dental issues. Left untreated, TMJ discomfort often gets worse. Slowly, then all at once.
