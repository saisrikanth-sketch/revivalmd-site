## What Is Microneedling and How Does It Work

Microneedling is a procedure that uses fine needles to create tiny channels in the treatment area. These signals tell your body to repair itself. Over time, new collagen forms and supports smoother skin and better skin tone.

Here’s the thing. Physicians control needle depth and speed. That matters because every skin type heals differently. Devices like SkinPen microneedling are used for precision and safety.

According to the American Academy of Dermatology, collagen induction therapy can improve uneven skin tone and enlarged pores when done as a series. It also improves absorption of serums, including growth factors and platelet rich plasma. Small steps. Real biology at work.

## Microneedling vs RF Microneedling

Microneedling vs RF microneedling comes down to depth and added energy. Both support collagen production. RF adds heat to reach deeper layers.

Which is better? Honestly? It depends. Laxity, acne scarring depth, and goals all matter. A consult helps decide. Some people benefit more from an RF microneedling treatment when tightening is part of the goal.

### Traditional Microneedling

Traditional microneedling treatments work well for early wrinkles, mild acne scarring, and uneven skin tone. Downtime is usually minimal downtime, with redness fading in a day or two.

Results build over time. That’s the point. This option is often paired with platelet rich plasma or calming serums. It may also be spaced into a plan that includes a chemical peel.

### RF Microneedling Treatment

RF-based options send controlled heat into deeper layers. That heat supports new collagen and firmness.

This approach is often chosen for looser areas like the jawline. Downtime can be slightly longer, but it still avoids surgery and supports optimal results.
