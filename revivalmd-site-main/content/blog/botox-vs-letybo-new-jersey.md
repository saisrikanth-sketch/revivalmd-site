## What Are Neuromodulators and How Do They Work

Neuromodulators are injectable treatments made from botulinum toxin type. Botox and Letybo are both forms of botulinum toxin type A. Their active ingredient works by limiting nerve signals that trigger muscle contraction.

When targeted facial muscles relax, the skin above them smooths. This reduces dynamic wrinkles like forehead lines, forehead creases, and crow’s feet. Over time, this can also help prevent deeper facial wrinkles.

These treatments go beyond cosmetics. According to the American Academy of Dermatology, botulinum toxin is FDA-approved for medical uses including migraine, excessive sweating, and muscle spasm disorders. The same science applies, just different treatment goals.

## Botox Overview History Uses and Benefits

Botox is the most established botulinum toxin type A in the U.S. It earned FDA approval for cosmetic use in 2002 and remains a fda approved neuromodulator with decades of clinical trials behind it.

Common treatment areas include forehead wrinkles, frown lines, and around the eyes. These areas move all day. Controlled relaxation can soften a wrinkle without changing your facial expression.

Botox also supports medical care. It’s FDA-approved for chronic migraine, with many migraine patients reporting fewer headache days. It’s also approved for hyperhidrosis, showing about 82 to 87 percent sweat reduction in studies. That’s real medicine, not just aesthetics.

## Letybo Overview and What Makes It Different

Letybo is a newer botulinum toxin type A option in the United States and is often referred to as korean botox because of its long history of use in South Korea. Letybo FDA approval in the U.S. followed years of international data and clinical trials.
